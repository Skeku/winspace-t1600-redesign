Riders are talking

Pitch your partner

We know. Sometimes a new bike is a want dressed up as a need. Five openers, field-tested by riders who made it home with the bike. Pick your angle, copy it, good luck.

Copy message

The full-disclosure angle

I have thought about this for four months. I have a spreadsheet. Column F is titled 'reasons' and it goes down to row 34. The bike weighs 7.3 kilograms. The spreadsheet, emotionally, weighs more.

Let me close the tab. Reserve the bike, delete the spreadsheet, everyone wins.

The romantic angle

Remember when you said I should do more things that make me happy? It is called Obsidian Gold, it is beautiful, and it makes me ridiculously happy. Also it is quieter than a drum kit and cheaper than redoing the kitchen.

I already know which wall it goes on. You will barely notice it. I will notice it constantly.

The health angle

The doctor said more cardio. This is cardio with a five-star review. Faster bike, longer rides, better sleep, better mood. You get back a happier, pleasantly tired person who volunteers to cook on Sundays.

The bike pays for itself in endorphins. Ask my future self.

The financial angle

Let's talk numbers. Spin bikes become clothes racks. Watches sit in drawers. This is 7.3 kilograms of carbon that holds its resale value and replaces a gym membership, a therapist and at least two streaming subscriptions.

It's not spending. It's consolidating.

The practical angle

Hear me out. It weighs 7.3 kilograms, which means I climb faster, get home earlier and complain less at dinner. It is black and gold, so it matches everything we own. And you can track my rides live, which, let's be honest, you already do.

The Winspace T1600 Ultra. Cheaper than a midlife crisis, and it looks better in the hallway.
